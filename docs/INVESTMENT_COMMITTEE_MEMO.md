# Investment Committee Memo - Project Redwood

## Executive view
Project Redwood is a fictional take-private case designed around bid discipline. The seller is asking **8.5x LTM EBITDA**. At that price, the base case produces **2.74x gross MOIC** and **22.3% gross IRR** over a five-year hold. The MOIC clears the 2.5x hurdle, but the IRR does not clear the 25% hurdle.

The deterministic reverse LBO supports a maximum entry multiple of approximately **8.1x**, implying a **0.4x / ~$24.5mm EV bid gap** versus the ask. A 10,000-path synthetic underwriting simulation further suggests approximately **8.0x** for a 50% probability of clearing 25% IRR and **7.5x** for a 75% probability.

## Transaction structure
- LTM revenue: **$325.0mm**
- LTM EBITDA: **$61.8mm**
- Seller ask: **8.5x EV / EBITDA**
- Enterprise value: **$524.9mm**
- Gross leverage: **5.0x**
- Sponsor equity: **$223.4mm**
- Hold period: **5 years**
- Exit assumption: **9.5x EV / EBITDA**

## Base-case returns
- Gross MOIC: **2.74x**
- Gross IRR: **22.3%**
- Exit net debt: **$170.5mm**
- Minimum interest coverage: **2.7x**

The capital structure remains serviceable in the deterministic case. The key debate is therefore not whether the business can service the debt, but whether the sponsor is paying too much relative to the required equity return.

## Reverse-LBO conclusion
The 25% IRR hurdle is the binding return constraint. Solving backwards from the sponsor exit proceeds gives a maximum deterministic bid of approximately **8.1x EBITDA**.

If the sponsor insists on paying 8.5x, the model shows that one of the following isolated improvements would be required to reach the 25% IRR hurdle:

- approximately **9.1% higher exit EBITDA**;
- approximately **10.4x exit EV / EBITDA** instead of 9.5x; or
- approximately **$76.3mm additional debt paydown**.

These are alternative levers, not assumptions that are meant to occur simultaneously.

## Downside and probability framing
The deterministic downside case produces approximately **10.6% gross IRR / 1.65x MOIC**. The synthetic simulation produces a median gross IRR of roughly **22.2%** and a 5th percentile gross IRR of roughly **10.8%**.

At the 8.5x asking price, only **32.9%** of simulated paths clear the 25% gross IRR hurdle. Under the synthetic assumptions, interest coverage falls below 2.0x in approximately **0.6%** of paths and an RCF draw occurs in approximately **0.1%**.

## Investment committee framing
The case does not suggest that 8.5x is impossible. It shows that 8.5x relies on a stronger outcome than the sponsor's selected hurdle would normally justify. The model therefore frames three rational choices:

1. reduce the bid;
2. identify credible operating / deleveraging upside not captured in the base case; or
3. explicitly accept a lower expected return / lower margin of safety.

For this fictional case, the most defensible price is closer to **8.0x-8.1x** under the selected return framework, with a lower bid required for a higher probability of clearing the hurdle.

## Disclosure
All assumptions, company data and transaction terms are fictional and created for educational purposes. This memo is not an investment recommendation.
